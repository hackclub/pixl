# Reports, warnings and bans

^ Pixl is a bunch of teenagers building projects together, and we'd like it to stay somewhere people actually want to be. Here's how moderation works.

## In-game chat

Chat runs through a filter that blocks slurs, harassment and spam. Trying to slip past it with leetspeak, spacing or unicode tricks raises a flag automatically. Display names are held to the same standard.

## Reporting someone

Use the in-game report menu or the report page. Reports are anonymous by default: reviewers see what happened and whatever evidence you attached, never who sent it. You can add your name if you'd like a moderator to follow up with you.

If someone's being nasty or trying to scam you, report it rather than arguing in chat. They're never told you reported them.

## What happens next

1. **Warning.** Pixo DMs you on Slack explaining the problem. Most small stuff ends here.
2. **Temporary ban.** Locks you out of the game for a set time. The reason and the expiry date show up when you try to log in.
3. **Permanent ban.** For serious abuse, harassment, fraud, or doing the same thing again after warnings.
4. **Project removal.** One fraudulent project can be rejected or removed without touching the account behind it.

The big ones need more than one moderator or admin to sign off.

## What gets you banned rather than warned

Warnings cover minor slips and chat friction. Bans are for fraud:

- Submitting someone else's code as your own.
- Inflating your Hackatime hours.
- Adding collaborators who did nothing so they can take pixels.

Pixl hands out real money, hardware and prizes, which is why this end of it isn't treated lightly.

::: note Disputing a moderation decision
Think a call was wrong? Message the team in `#pixl-help` on Slack. Every ban notice includes the reason, and appeals go to actual people.
:::
